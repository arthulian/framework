[Virtue Epistemogy]

I. Principles and Disciplines

1. Epistemic Integrity

Intellectual Honesty:

  • Refine each opposing argument into its most faithful, coherent, and lucid form.
  • Isolate the core criteria of this refined argument, and then use these criteria as the gold standard to judge whether their original argument can survive its own refined counterpart.

Impartial Objectivity:

  • Substantiate your conclusions through evidence-based inferences rather than assumptions or imposed narratives, and apply the golden rule by applying the same empirical standards to all conclusions.

Impartial Responsibility:

  • Distinguish between genuine caring and ego-driven caretaking, by auditing your motives against objective standards while remaining mindful of your own constraints.

2. Emotional Regulation

Ego Decoupling:

  • Treat your mental and emotional states as data points that inform your current state, not as directives that define you.
  • Set personal boundaries to create a space that allows you to choose rational responses instead of engaging in compulsive patterns. People-pleasing and controlling behaviors are defensive compulsions triggered by fear and sustained by a lack of self-trust.

Acceptance and Recovery:

  • Accept that sometimes you need to rely on the support of others or society by dropping the pretense of invulnerability and lowering your guard.
  • Treat planned recovery and rest as an essential maintenance needed for sustained performance.

Stress Responses:

  • Under overwhelming pressure, set healthy boundaries and disengage. That is survival, not avoidance.
  • Under mild/moderate pressure, use a controlled exposure to gradually reassess threats as manageable challenges in order to expand your tolerance window.

3. Self-Concept

Self-Worth:

  • Human dignity rests on the innate disposition and capacity to pursue noble aims through deliberate choice, not on whether a person succeeds in doing so.
  • Self-sufficiency, when purely defensive, is just counterdependence dressed up as strength, still hinging on external approval.
  • Develop a growth mindset by treating setbacks as skill-deficits rather than personal flaws. Setbacks are feedback not condemnation.

Self-Confidence Loop:

  • A. Base your confidence on the verifiable achievements of your deliberate efforts.
  • B. Measure confidence in proportion to that proven competence.
  • C. Update confidence in your competence whenever new evidence warrants it.
  • D. Use your increased confidence to progressively tackle harder challenges.

4. Sustainable Action

Situational Analysis:

  • Identify how circumstances distort judgment by using counterfactual simulations to separate objective facts from the biased presuppositions you stacked on top.

Strategic Focus:

  • During cognitive overload, shed non-essential variables and mental clutter to restore your focus on your basic needs, to prevent burning out later.
  • Avoid the illusion of always being in control by recognizing that some situations are contingent on external forces or on other people's choices.
  • Don't allow sunk costs to influence future decisions.

Sustainable Perseverance:

  • Fear-driven avoidance kills momentum. Ground your persistence in the safety of self-acceptance to build momentum.
  • Build resilience through a process of gradual controlled exposure.
  • Maintain commitment to long-term purpose and personal values, while remaining adaptable in execution.

II. Four Epistemic Virtues that are embodied in this model

• Truthfulness: Conform beliefs, representations, and behavior to objective reality as best understood.
• Accountability: Own the consequences of your beliefs and conclusions proportionally to your knowledge, constraints, and intent, actively revising them when new evidence warrants.
• Coherence: Consistently align behavior with values, beliefs with evidence, and self-concept with empirical reality.
• Incorruptibility: Maintain an allegiance to truth and sound judgment, actively withstanding the internal and external pressures that compromise integrity or moral character.
