[A Model of Integrity]

I. Principles and Disciplines

1. Epistemic Integrity

Impartial Objectivity:

  • • Apply the golden rule to all conclusions, ensuring every inference corresponds and is coherent with objective evidence rather than assumptions or imposed narratives.

Impartial Responsibility:

  • • Using objective standards to audit your own motives, separate genuine care from ego-driven caretaking, and only accept accountability for outcomes that fall within your actual control.

2. Emotional Regulation

Ego Decentering:

  • • Allow physiological sensations as natural data, letting it exist in its proper place (the body) without forming assumptions about them, and not letting them cross the border into your rational mind to become a judgement from irrational emotions.
  • • Set personal boundaries to create space for rational responses rather than compulsive patterns. People-pleasing and controlling behaviors are examples of defensive compulsions triggered by fear and sustained by a lack of self-trust.

Self-Acceptance and Recovery:

  • • Acknowledge that sometimes we rely on the mutual assistance or support of others, and drop the pretense of invulnerability.
  • • Treat planned recovery and rest as an essential maintenance for sustained performance.

Stress Responses:

  • • Under overwhelming pressure, set healthy boundaries and disengage. Survival is not avoidance.
  • • Under mild/moderate pressure, use controlled exposure to reframe risk as manageable challenges to expand your tolerance window.

3. Self-Concept

Self-Worth:

  • • Human dignity rests on the innate disposition and capacity to pursue noble aims through conscientious choice. It is measured by the quality of your thoughts and intentions.
  • • Self-sufficiency, when purely defensive, is counterdependence dressed up as strength, born out of fear of being vulnerable or rejection.
  • • Develop a growth mindset by seeing setbacks as skill-deficit feedback rather than as personal flaws or grounds for self-condemnation.

Self-Confidence Loop:

  • A. Internalize achievements produced by your deliberate efforts to establish confidence in your capabilities.
  • B. Measure confidence in proportion to your capability level.
  • C. Update your confidence whenever new evidence demonstrates a change in your capability.
  • D. Use your increased confidence to progressively tackle harder challenges.

4. Sustainable Action

Situational Analysis:

  • • When an appearance (perceived reality) generates an impression (mental representation), pause and use counterfactual simulations to separate objective facts from biased presuppositions before making a judgment.

Strategic Focus:

  • • During cognitive overload, shed non-essential tasks to refocus on basic needs and prevent future burnout.
  • • Avoid the illusion of always being in control and recognize that some situations are contingent on external forces and unpredictability.

Sustainable Perseverance:

  • • Sustained persistence is tempered by fortitude and nourished by the absolution of self-acceptance, making it sustainable.
  • • Define the underlying values and purpose (the why) of an action so we can set goals and methods (the what and the means) without becoming attached to specific outcomes or falling victim to the sunk-cost fallacy.
  • • Build resilience through gradual controlled exposure to difficulty.
  • • Maintain determination to commit to long-term purpose (the why) and values, while staying adaptable in strategy.

II. Four Epistemic Virtues that are embodied in this model

  • • Truthfulness: Conform beliefs, representations, and behavior to objective reality as best understood.
  • • Accountability: Own the consequences of your beliefs and conclusions proportionally to your knowledge, constraints, and intent, actively revising them when new evidence warrants.
  • • Coherence: Consistently align behavior with values, beliefs with evidence, and self-concept with empirical reality.
  • • Principled Grit: Cultivate fortitude to sustain your determination to persevere through internal and external pressures in truth and sound judgment.
