[A Model of Integrity]

Intended Application:
  • • To resolve the discrepancies within ourselves in order to preserve our integrity while operating under high-stakes performance conditions.

I. Principles and Disciplines

1. Epistemic Hygiene

Credibility Appraisal:

  • Base confidence in tried-and-true results.
  • Reduce confidence level when predictions fail.

Situational Analysis:

  • Use counterfactual simulations to isolate facts from assumptions and mental projections, to identify how circumstances distort judgment.

2. Epistemic Integrity

Mirror-Testing the Strongest Case:

  • Steelman counterarguments before reaching your own conclusions.
  • Mirror-test your steelmanned criteria by revising those original conclusions that cannot survive their own criteria.

Impartial Objectivity:

  • Derive your conclusions directly from evidence-driven inferences rather than assumptions or mental projections, ensuring the same empirical and rational standards are applied to every claim.

Personal Accountability:

  • Hold your own motives and limitations to the standards of impartial objectivity and apply these standards independently of whether your conditions are favorable or unfavorable. Take full ownership of your choices and their consequences.

3. Emotional Regulation

Ego Decoupling:

  • Tag thoughts and emotions as non-binding data points rather than components of the self, but ensure you don't invalidate core values.
  • Set personal boundaries to create a space (the ability to choose) between impulsive drives and responses, allowing for rational choices.

Acceptance and Recovery:

  • In order to enable adaptation, accept human limitations such as admitting there are periods of situational dependence or reliance on others.
  • Planned recovery should be viewed as essential for a sustained performance.

Stress Responses:

  • Under high pressure, disengage through healthy boundaries.
  • Under mild/moderate pressure, use a controlled exposure to expand your stress-tolerance window.

4. Self-Concept

Self-Worth:

  • intrinsic self-worth is solely derived from your human capacity to deliberate, and your volition to align to the "four values that support integrity".
  • Retain an internal locus of control by rejecting self-worth based on counter-dependent self-sufficiency, which is dependent on praise/criticism and expectations.
  • Treat setbacks as skill deficit feedback rather than personal flaws that diminish your self-worth.

Self-Confidence:

  • Base your confidence on the verifiable achievements of your deliberate efforts.
  • Measure confidence in proportion to that proven competence.
  • Revise confidence in your assumptions whenever new evidence warrants it.
  • Use your increased confidence to progressively tackle harder challenges.

5. Sustainable Action

Strategic Focus:

  • Low-Power Cognition: under heavy-load, shed non-essential tasks to keep your focus on things that offer the greatest utility towards your purpose.
  • Avoid the illusion of always being in control of your life, and recognize that sometimes situations are contingent on heteronomy.
  • Don't allow sunk costs to influence future decisions.

Sustainable Perseverance:

  • Counter fear-driven avoidance by grounding persistence in self-kindness (self-kindness to create safety, and persistence to build momentum).
  • Build low-fragility through a gradual managed exposure.
  • Maintain commitment to long-term purpose while remaining adaptable in execution.

II. The Four Values that support Integrity

• Truthfulness: Conform beliefs, representations, and actions to objective reality as best understood.
• Responsibility: Determine the proportionality between intention, knowledge, agency, and consequences, bounded by one's own limiting constraints.
• Coherence: Consistently align behavior with values, beliefs with evidence, and self-concept with empirical reality.
• Incorruptibility: Resist influences that compromise truth, judgment, integrity, or moral character.
