[Framework]

Primary Objective:
  • • To regulate the integrity of appraisal, reason, emotion, self-concept, and adaptive action, under high-pressure conditions.

I. Method Library

1. Appraisal Integrity

Reality-Tested Credibility:

  • Anchor claims in time-tested reliability.
  • Reduce confidence when predictions fail.
Embodies: Truthfulness, Wisdom

Belief Updating:

  • Adjust belief confidence according to the available evidence.
  • Distinguish between observation, inference, and claims.
  • Prioritize empirical accuracy by revising beliefs when evidence warrants it.
Embodies: Truthfulness, Coherence

Bias Mitigation:

  • Consider allocentric perspectives before making judgments.
  • Use counterfactual 'What ifs' to distinguish between facts and feelings.
Embodies: Incorruptibility

2. Intellectual Integrity

Mirror-Testing the Strongest Case:

  • Steelman opposing positions before reaching conclusions.
  • Reject conclusions that cannot withstand those strongest counterarguments.
Embodies: Wisdom, Truthfulness

Impartiality and Objectivity:

  • Prioritize factual accuracy over self-justification.
  • Apply consistent standards of verification.
Embodies: Incorruptibility, Justice

Contextual Intent:

  • Evaluate both intent and consequences within their situational context.
  • Balance between situational rationale and personal accountability.
Embodies: Justice

3. Emotional Regulation

Grounded Awareness:

  • Tag thoughts and emotions as transient data rather than components of the self.
  • Reduce emotional interference without invalidating core values.
  • Choose deliberate responses instead of impulsive reactions.
Embodies: Wisdom

Acceptance and Recovery:

  • Recognize vulnerability as universal.
  • Accept present limitations in order to enable adaptation.
  • View planned recovery as essential for sustained performance.
Embodies: Justice

Stress Response:

  • Under high pressure, disengage through healthy boundaries.
  • Under moderate pressure, use controlled exposure to build resilience.
  • Increase challenge gradually by matching challenge level to current capability.

4. Self-Concept

Self-Worth:

  • Base intrinsic self-worth on the deliberate and volitional development of moral character.
  • Reject self-worth based on praise or criticism that comes from defensive competence or performative self-sufficiency.
Embodies: Coherence, Incorruptibility

Empirical Confidence:

  • Measure confidence in proportion to validated competence.
  • Base your confidence on the verifiable outcomes of your deliberate efforts.
  • Use increased confidence to tackle progressively harder challenges.
Embodies: Truthfulness

Setbacks as Skill Feedback:

  • Treat setbacks as evidence of skill deficits rather than diminished self-worth.
Embodies: Justice

5. Adaptive Action

Strategic Focus:

  • Low-Power Cognition: under heavy-load, shed non-essential tasks to keep your focus on things that offer the greatest utility towards your purpose.
  • Avoid the illusion of total control.
  • Don't allow sunk costs to influence future decisions.
Embodies: Wisdom

Sustainable Persistence:

  • Build resilience through gradual exposure.
  • Balance persistence with self-kindness to prevent burnout.
Embodies: Coherence, Justice

Dynamic Execution:

  • Maintain commitment to long-term purpose while remaining flexible in execution.
Embodies: Coherence

II. The Five Values Embodying the Methods

• Truthfulness: Align beliefs, representations, and actions with reality as best understood.
• Justice/Fairness: Contextually weigh between a person's intent and the consequences of his actions.
• Wisdom: Apply knowledge, experience, and judgment toward beneficial action.
• Coherence: Rational consistency that preserves intellectual integrity by aligning actions with values, updating beliefs based on outcomes, and integrating self-concept with empirical evidence.
• Incorruptibility: Resist influences that compromise truth, judgment, integrity, or moral character.
