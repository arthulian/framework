Epistemic Integrity

Impartial Objectivity

  • Apply the golden rule to each conclusion, ensuring that every inference is coherent with objective evidence rather than assumptions or imposed narratives.

Impartial Responsibility

  • Use objective standards to audit your own motives, distinguish genuine care from ego-driven caretaking, and accept accountability only for outcomes within your control.
2

Emotional Regulation

Ego Decentering

  • Treat physiological sensations as natural data, allowing them to exist in the body (their proper place) without making assumptions about them or allowing them to become conclusions driven by irrational emotions.
  • Establish personal boundaries that create space for rational responses rather than compulsive patterns.
Examples of defensive compulsions: people-pleasing (submitting) and controlling behaviours (manipulating) — triggered by fear of abandonment.

Self-Acceptance & Recovery

  • Recognize that people sometimes rely on mutual assistance and support from others, and relinquish the pretense of invulnerability.
  • Treat planned recovery and rest as essential maintenance for sustained performance.

Stress Responses

  • Under overwhelming pressure: establish healthy boundaries and disengage. Survival is not avoidance.
  • Under mild or moderate pressure: use controlled exposure to reframe risks as manageable challenges and expand your tolerance window.
3

Self-Concept

Self-Worth

  • Human dignity rests on the innate disposition and capacity to pursue noble aims through conscientious choices. It is measured by the quality of one’s thoughts and intentions.
  • When purely defensive, self-sufficiency is counterdependence presented as strength and rooted in fear of vulnerability.
  • Develop a growth mindset by viewing setbacks as skill-deficit feedback rather than as personal flaws or grounds for self-condemnation.

Self-Confidence Loop

4

Sustainable Action

Situational Analysis

  • When an appearance (perceived reality) generates an impression (mental representation), pause and use counterfactual simulations to distinguish objective facts from false presuppositions.

Strategic Focus

  • During cognitive overload, set aside nonessential tasks to refocus on basic needs and prevent future burnout.
  • Avoid the illusion of complete control; recognize that some situations depend on external forces and are inherently unpredictable.

Sustainable Perseverance

  • Sustained persistence is tempered by fortitude and nourished by the tolerance of self-acceptance, making it more durable.
  • Define the underlying values and purpose (the "why") of an action to establish goals and methods (the "what" and "how") without becoming attached to specific outcomes or falling prey to the sunk-cost fallacy.
  • Build resilience through gradual, controlled exposure to difficulty.
  • Remain committed to long-term purpose and values (the "why") while adapting strategies as needed.
