Framework

Primary Objective
  • • Regulate the integrity of perception, self-concept, reasoning, and action.
  • • Maintain effective functioning under high-pressure conditions.

I. Method Library

Reusable methods organized by the domain they serve.

1. Perceptual Integrity

Reality-Tested Credibility

  • Anchor claims in historical reliability.
  • Reject claims when predictions fail.
Embodies: Truthfulness, Wisdom

Belief Updating

  • Update beliefs in proportion to the available evidence.
  • Distinguish between observation, inference, and claims.
  • Prioritize empirical accuracy over maintaining an interpretive narrative.
  • Scale conviction only to the strength of supporting evidence.
Embodies: Truthfulness, Coherence

Bias Mitigation

  • Consider allocentric perspectives before making judgments.
  • Use counterfactual reasoning to reconcile facts with feelings.
Embodies: Incorruptibility

2. Cognitive Integrity

Mirror-Testing the Steelman

  • Consider the strongest opposing arguments before reaching conclusions.
  • Reject conclusions that cannot withstand those strongest counterarguments.
Embodies: Wisdom, Truthfulness

Objectivity and Impartiality

  • Never compromise objective truth to protect self-image.
  • Apply consistent standards of verification.
  • Prioritize factual accuracy over self-justification.
Embodies: Incorruptibility, Justice/Fairness

Contextual Intent

  • Evaluate both intent and consequences within their situational context.
  • Balance a situational understanding with personal accountability.
Embodies: Justice/Fairness

3. Emotional Regulation

Grounded Awareness

  • Maintain an observer perspective.
  • Tag thoughts and emotions as transient data rather than components of the self.
  • Reduce emotional interference without invalidating emotion.
  • Choose deliberate responses instead of impulsive reactions.
Embodies: Wisdom, Actualization

Acceptance and Recovery

  • Recognize vulnerability as universal.
  • Accept present limitations in order to enable adaptation.
  • View planned recovery as essential for sustained performance.
Embodies: Justice/Fairness, Actualization

Calibrated Stress Response

  • Under high pressure, disengage through healthy boundaries.
  • Under moderate pressure, use controlled exposure to build resilience.
  • Increase challenge gradually as capacity grows.
  • Match challenge level to current capability.
Embodies: Actualization

4. Self-Concept

Self-Worth

  • Base intrinsic self-worth on the deliberate development of moral character.
  • Reject self-worth based on praise or criticism that comes from defensive competence or performative self-sufficiency.
Embodies: Coherence, Incorruptibility

Empirical Confidence

  • Define confidence as a measure of validated competence.
  • Base confidence on outcomes produced by deliberate effort.
  • Align confidence to reality using verifiable feedback.
  • Use increased confidence to tackle progressively harder challenges.
Embodies: Truthfulness

Setbacks as Skill Feedback

  • Treat setbacks as evidence of skill deficits rather than diminished self-worth.
Embodies: Justice/Fairness

5. Adaptive Action

Strategic Focus

  • Focus effort toward what is within your control.
  • Avoid the illusion of total control.
  • Base decisions on present utility rather than sunk costs.
Embodies: Wisdom, Actualization

Sustainable Persistence

  • Build perseverance through gradual exposure.
  • Balance persistence with self-kindness to prevent burnout.
Embodies: Coherence, Justice/Fairness

Dynamic Execution

  • Maintain commitment to long-term purpose while remaining flexible in execution.
Embodies: Coherence

The Six Values Embodying the Methods

Truthfulness Align beliefs, representations, and actions with reality as best understood.
Justice/Fairness Contextually weighs between a person's intent and the consequences of his actions.
Wisdom Apply knowledge, experience, and judgment toward beneficial action.
Coherence Maintain logical consistency among beliefs, values, reasoning, self-concept, and behavior.
Actualization Continually develop one's highest capacities through intentional growth.
Incorruptibility Resist influences that compromise truth, judgment, integrity, or moral character.

II. Functional System

Functional components that use methods from the Method Library.

I. Perception

Acquire and interpret information.

  • ◇Guided By
    Truthfulness, Wisdom
  • ◇Requires
    Reality-Tested Credibility, Observation, Belief Updating
  • ◇Uses
    Reality-Tested Credibility, Belief Updating, Bias Mitigation
  • ◇Pipeline
    Observe → Compare sources → Evaluate credibility → Detect bias → Update beliefs
  • ◇Feeds Into
    Reasoning
II. Reasoning

Evaluate and integrate information to produce justified conclusions.

  • ◇Guided By
    Truthfulness, Wisdom, Coherence, Justice/Fairness, Incorruptibility
  • ◇Requires
    Evidence, Belief Updating, Contextual Intent
  • ◇Uses
    Mirror-Testing the Steelman, Objectivity and Impartiality, Contextual Intent
  • ◇Pipeline
    Interpret → Analyze → Compare → Evaluate impartially → Test assumptions → Integrate context → Form conclusions
  • ◇Feeds Into
    Emotional Regulation • Action
III. Emotional Regulation

Manage thoughts, emotions, and behavior in service of principled action.

  • ◇Guided By
    Wisdom, Justice/Fairness, Actualization
  • ◇Requires
    Grounded Awareness, Self-Examination, Reasoning
  • ◇Uses
    Grounded Awareness, Acceptance and Recovery, Calibrated Stress Response
  • ◇Pipeline
    Monitor → Regulate emotion → Recover → Recalibrate
  • ◇Feeds Into
    Self-Concept • Action
IV. Self-Concept

Integrate beliefs, values, and character into a stable self-concept.

  • ◇Guided By
    Coherence, Justice/Fairness, Incorruptibility
  • ◇Requires
    Self-Examination, Updated Capability, Emotional Regulation
  • ◇Uses
    Self-Worth, Empirical Confidence, Failure as Skill Feedback
  • ◇Pipeline
    Self-examine → Integrate experience → Align values → Preserve integrity
  • ◇Feeds Into
    Action
V. Action

Execute reasoned and principled decisions in the real world.

  • ◇Guided By
    Truthfulness, Wisdom, Coherence, Actualization
  • ◇Requires
    Reasoned Decisions, Stable Self-Concept, Emotional Regulation
  • ◇Uses
    Strategic Focus, Sustainable Persistence, Dynamic Execution
  • ◇Pipeline
    Plan → Prioritize → Execute → Monitor outcomes → Adjust strategy
  • ◇Feeds Into
    Adaptive Performance
VI. Adaptive Performance

Sustain and improve performance through evidence, learning, and adaptation.

  • ◇Guided By
    Truthfulness, Wisdom, Actualization
  • ◇Requires
    Outcomes from Action, Evidence from Perception, Stable Self-Concept
  • ◇Uses
    Feedback, Learning, Adaptation, Deliberate Practice
  • ◇Pipeline
    Evaluate outcomes → Identify errors → Update beliefs → Refine strategies → Reinforce effective practices
